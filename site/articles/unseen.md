# Shapes we cannot see

Every glyph here is a picture of something no one can look at directly: a loop in three
dimensions, flattened into ink. Physics is full of pictures like that. Two of them, the
electron's cloud and the hidden dimensions of string theory, turn out to be close relatives
of the glyphs. Every picture on this page is computed from the mathematics.

![A cross-section of a Calabi–Yau manifold: the surface z₁⁵ + z₂⁵ = 1, built by Andrew Hanson's method and shaded for this page.](fig:calabiyau)

## Where the electron might be

![Hydrogen orbitals, drawn point by point from exact solutions of the Schrödinger equation: 1s, 2s and 2p (top); two 3d and a 4f (bottom). Ink and sienna mark the two signs of the wavefunction.](fig:orbitals)

In 1926 Erwin Schrödinger wrote down the equation for the electron in a hydrogen atom, and
solved it exactly.[^schrodinger] That same year Max Born worked out what the answer means: it
does not say where the electron *is*, only how likely it is to be found at each place.[^born]

So an electron has no path, only a cloud. Each dot here is one place it might be found, drawn
at random in proportion to that likelihood.[^griffiths] Where the cloud thins to nothing, between
the shells of the 2s or between the lobes of a 3d, the electron is never found at all.

The glyphs are the opposite: one definite path, with nothing uncertain about it.

**Look further:** [atomic orbitals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atomic_orbital); a
[3D quantum atom simulator](https://github.com/Nazat02/Quantum-Atom) that samples the clouds
of every element; and the 2013 experiment that photographed the nodes of a hydrogen atom's
cloud directly.[^stodolna]

## The sphere's own patterns

![Four spherical harmonics, with the 13 points of the glyphs on them. Sienna and blue are the two signs; the thin lines are where each pattern is zero.](fig:harmonics)

The lobes of an orbital come from **spherical harmonics**: the natural patterns of a sphere,
the way a drumhead has its own modes of vibration. Any pattern on a sphere can be built out of
them, from the shapes of orbitals to maps of the cosmic microwave background.[^harmonics]

The sphere connects back to the glyphs in another way. In 1904, J. J. Thomson pictured the
atom as electrons scattered through a ball of positive charge, and asked how they would
arrange themselves.[^thomson] How *N* repelling points settle on a sphere is now called the
**Thomson problem**. It is a close cousin of the Tammes problem, which places the 13 points
every glyph is drawn through.

**Look further:** [spherical harmonics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spherical_harmonics),
the [Thomson problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomson_problem), and the Tammes problem
in [*Thirty million loops*](../loops/#from-a-circle-to-a-sphere).

## Six dimensions, curled up

![The same construction for z₁ⁿ + z₂ⁿ = 1 with n = 2 to 7. At n = 5 it is a slice of the quintic Calabi–Yau manifold.](fig:cyfamily)

String theory needs ten dimensions. Four are the space and time we know; the other six are
curled up too small to see. To work, they must curl into a particular kind of shape, a
**Calabi–Yau manifold**.[^chsw] Eugenio Calabi conjectured in the 1950s that such shapes
exist,[^calabi] and Shing-Tung Yau proved it in 1977.[^yau]

No one can draw a six-dimensional shape. In 1994 Andrew Hanson found a way to draw a slice of
one: a surface that lives in four dimensions, shown in three, and printed on a flat page.[^hanson]
His pictures became the standard image of string theory's hidden dimensions.

**Look further:** Hanson's own [interactive version](https://demonstrations.wolfram.com/CalabiYauSpace/)
on Wolfram Demonstrations;[^hansonwolfram] an [interactive gallery](https://analyticphysics.com/Higher%20Dimensions/Visualizing%20Calabi-Yau%20Manifolds.htm)
that steps through the exponents;[^analytic] and Bathsheba Grossman's
[Calabi–Yau in glass](https://bathsheba.com/crystal/calabiyau/), a sculpture laser-etched
inside a crystal cube.[^bathsheba]

## Shadows

![One loop, C4, seen from three directions: three different glyphs from one object.](fig:shadows)

All of these pictures are shadows. An orbital is a three-dimensional probability drawn on a
flat page. A Calabi–Yau picture is a two-dimensional slice of six dimensions, seen in three
and printed in two.

A glyph is a loop in three dimensions, turned to one viewpoint and inked. Turn it, and the
same loop writes a different glyph. Try it in the [explorer](../spike/).
